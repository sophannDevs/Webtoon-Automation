## 요약 (Summary)

Top 5 mechanisms for an evidence-and-exposure school revenge story, each with its one-line working principle:

1. **Wound -> Witnessed proof -> Vow (EP1 hook spine).** Show the injustice concretely, then show that the hero
   now *holds something* (proof, access, knowledge), then a quiet vow. Works because the reader's anger is converted
   into a promise of payoff in the same episode. [Observed in The Glory, Revenge of Others, Viral Hit premises; spine is Hypothesis]
2. **The payoff-promise cliffhanger.** EP1 must end not on more suffering but on the first visible move of the plan
   (a reveal that the hero is closer to the bully than anyone knows, or that the hero has the evidence). Unresolved
   *outcome*, resolved *intent*. [Craft + Observed; The Glory Part 1 ending is the model]
3. **The control-flip reveal.** A scene staged so the bully believes they are winning; the final beats reveal the hero
   prepared this moment in advance (recorded, witnessed, timed). Reader re-reads the whole scene in a new light.
   Works by showing true information early and letting the reader and the bully draw the wrong conclusion. [Craft]
4. **Hitchcock's bomb, used in reverse order.** Alternate two kinds of episodes: in some, tell the reader the plan
   (suspense: we know, the bully does not); in others, hide it and reveal at the end (surprise). Suspense carries
   the middle; surprise carries the ending. [Craft: Hitchcock/Truffaut]
5. **Plant three, flip one.** Each twist gets at least three earlier panels that change meaning after the reveal;
   one per episode is the twist, the others are seeds for later. A twist with no plant is a "cheap twist".
   [Craft rule of thumb; counts are not established standards]

### 1.2 Extracted formula (order matters)

Recommended EP1 beat order for a 50+ panel episode. Panel ranges are proportional targets. [Hypothesis built from
the table above plus the short-form 3-second / 7-second hook model, which is a vertical-video rule of thumb, not webtoon data.]

| Step | Panels (of ~55) | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 Cold open "aftermath" | 1-3 | Flash-forward or in-media-res: a silent, frozen face, a crowd looking at a phone (screen blank), one line of dialogue | A question in the first scroll: "what happened / what will happen?" |
| H2 The wound, specific | 4-14 | Bullying shown through words, faces, silence: names, a rule of the class hierarchy (a vote, a group chat, a "duty") | Anger + clarity on *who* is guilty. Specific names beat vague cruelty (The Glory's dropout letter) |
| H3 The system fails | 15-22 | Teacher / parent / rule protects the bully | Removes the "easy fix", justifies self-made justice |
| H4 The hero's edge | 23-32 | Not strength: a habit (always recording, perfect memory, access to a record, being underestimated) planted as if it were a weakness | First **plant**. The reader should read it as pathetic, later as power |
| H5 The bottom | 33-40 | The worst humiliation of the episode; the hero goes silent | Max tension; sets the "debt" the series must repay |
| H6 The turn | 41-48 | Alone, the hero does one thing that shows intent (saves a file, labels a folder, writes five names, notices one detail) | Converts despair to vow. Short, dialogue-light, quiet |
| H7 Payoff-promise cliffhanger | 49-55+ | See section 2.2 | Forces "next episode" |

Principles:
- Show the wound with **names and rules**, not with injury. This suits the 15+ brief and Khmer-edition art (faces, silence).
- **Explain the world as little as possible.** One rule of the school hierarchy is enough in EP1. [Craft, observation-frames A]
- **Within the first ~40% the reader must see a hint the hero is not helpless.** Weak Hero and Viral Hit both put the
  hero's edge into the premise. A pure-suffering EP1 is the classic "goguma" risk. Note the conflicting view:
  one Korean column argues readers recognise goguma but "only clench their fists, they do not leave". Both are cited;
  for EP1 I recommend the safe side (show the edge early). [Observed: seoulalien substack; ilyosisa]

## 클리프행어 메커니즘 (Cliffhanger mechanics)

### 2.1 Types, ranked by "next episode" pressure for this genre

| Type | Position | What is shown / hidden | Pressure | Fit for revenge-by-evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Control-flip line** ("I have all of it.") | Last 1-2 panels after a 3-panel build | Shown: hero has the upper hand. Hidden: what exactly, and when it lands | Strong | Best for EP1 end |
| **Proximity reveal** (hero is inside the bully's world: same club, their sibling's tutor, the teacher's new assistant) | Last panel, reaction of bully off-panel or not at all | Shown: position. Hidden: plan | Strong | The Glory Part 1 model (teacher of the bully's daughter) [Observed] |
| **Ally -> suspect flip** | Last 2-3 panels | Shown: one clue. Hidden: confirmation | Strong | Pyramid Game EP1 [Observed] |
| **New person in the secret** | Last panel | Shown: someone else knows. Hidden: whose side they are on | Medium-strong | Revenge of Others EP1 [Observed] |
| **Evidence about to drop** (finger over "send", file about to play in assembly) | Final panel cut before the result | Shown: the trigger. Hidden: the reaction | Strong but use rarely; repeated use reads as a cheat | Good for mid-series episodes |
| **Counter-attack** (bully finds the revenge board/evidence) | Last panel | Shown: danger to the hero | Strong | The Glory Part 1 ending (bully breaks in, finds the revenge wall) [Observed]; use at an arc end, not EP1 |
| More suffering, no move | Last panel | — | Weak / risky | Avoid as an EP1 ending (goguma risk) [Hypothesis] |

### 2.2 How to make EP1 end on a payoff-promise cliffhanger

Formula: **Resolved intent + unresolved outcome + one concrete object.**

1. Panels ~49-51: the bully's victory lap. They believe the matter is closed (a joke, a laugh, "nobody will believe you").
2. Panel ~52: hard cut to the hero alone. Silence panel.
3. Panel ~53: the **object** (the plant from H4) shown with new meaning: the phone that was "always out because he's a
   loser" was recording; the duty roster he was forced to keep is a dated log of every incident; the five names.
   Screens and paper are drawn blank (Khmer edition), so the meaning must come from **framing + one line**.
4. Panel ~54: a single line, low and calm. Pattern: *counts or dates, not threats*. E.g. "Day one." / "Forty-one days.
   Five names." / "Keep laughing. I'm recording." Threat-free lines keep it 15+ and read as control, not rage.
5. Panel ~55 (optional sting): the bully, elsewhere, gets a small anomaly (a message preview from an unknown sender,
   screen blank; a teacher asking them to stay after class). Promises the first domino next episode.

Why it works: the reader's need for justice (built in H2-H5) gets a *promise* with a visible tool, so the reader
clicks to see the tool used. Suspense comes from the reader knowing more than the bully. [Craft: Hitchcock; Hypothesis for webtoon]

Paywall note: in Free-if-You-Wait style models, a 2024 Korean journal analysis argues frequent cliffhangers and dense
episodes encourage paid early access (Solo Leveling vs Tower of God). This is a content analysis, not a reader study.
[Observed claim, effect Unverified]

## 반전 역설계 (Twist reverse-engineering)

### 3.1 Twist types for evidence-and-exposure school revenge

| Type | Reader's prediction that is flipped | Trigger | Best timing | Shock amplifier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Control flip** (trap reveal) | "The bully has cornered the hero." | Hero reveals the scene was staged / recorded / witnessed | Last 15% of an episode | Re-show an earlier panel of the same scene from a new angle (the angle reveals the hidden witness / camera) |
| **Ally betrayal** | "This friend is on the hero's side." | One small inconsistency becomes visible | End of an episode | Silent panel on the hero's face, no dialogue |
| **Secret ally** (reverse betrayal) | "This bully follower is loyal to the bully." | Follower hands the hero something | Mid or end | Cut from the bully trusting them to the handover |
| **Victim/perpetrator role swap** | "This person is a bystander / victim." | Evidence shows they started it | Arc end | Their own recorded words played back |
| **Hidden identity / position** | "The hero is nobody." | Hero's link to authority, money or the bully's family revealed | EP1 or EP2 end | Bully's polite greeting to the hero in front of others |
| **Motive reveal** | "Revenge is for himself." | It is for someone else (a sibling, a friend who left) | Late arc | Object planted in EP1 (a second name tag, a second chair) |
| **Rule twist** | "The system protects the bully." | The hero uses the system's own rule (school code, vote, scholarship clause) against them | Any | Teacher or adult says the rule out loud |
| **Counter-twist on the hero** | "The plan is perfect." | Bully already knew / has their own evidence | Midpoint of series | Use sparingly; it resets tension |

Fairness test (apply to each): **at least three earlier moments must take on new meaning after the reveal**; the
classic fair-play line is that *misdirection shows true information and lets the reader conclude wrongly, while
deception hides or falsifies it*. Twists that rely on deception are flagged **"cheap twist" (날림 반전)**.
[Craft: codidact writing Q&A, Writing Excuses "surprising yet inevitable", rivereditor guide; the "3 moments" count is a rule of thumb]

### 3.2 Anatomy of "the reveal that flips who is in control"

Mechanism (5 parts, in order):

1. **Stage the bully's win in full.** Give the bully the dialogue, the crowd, the last word. Readers must feel the loss.
2. **Hide the camera in plain sight.** The tool was shown earlier (H4) but framed as weakness or routine. This is
   misdirection by meaning, not by hiding. [Craft]
3. **The tell.** One panel where the hero is calmer than they should be. Readers who notice it feel clever later.
4. **The flip line.** The hero speaks the evidence out loud in front of the one audience that matters (teacher, parent,
   class, scholarship board). In a text-free image, the evidence is **heard**, not read: playback of the bully's own
   words in a speech bubble with a "device" tail, or a witness repeating them. [Hypothesis: production adaptation for Khmer edition]
5. **The reaction ladder.** Faces in order of power: crowd -> follower -> bully -> adult. The bully's face last (or
   withheld until the next episode). Silence panel after.

Why it satisfies (사이다) instead of feeling cruel: the bully is defeated by **their own words and acts**, the payoff
is proportional, and the hero does not gain by inventing anything. Reviewers' complaint about The Glory — that some
wins feel like Dong-eun "just got lucky" — shows the risk when the hero's prep is not planted on screen. [Observed: MyDramaList review; SF Chronicle praises the "blink-and-you'll-miss-it" clues]

### 3.3 One twist per episode — patterns

| Pattern | How | Load |
|---|---|---|
| **Domino chain** | Each episode exposes one bully / one secret; the exposure itself reveals the next target's weak spot | Light; reliable weekly sider |
| **Escalation ladder** | Twist size grows: object -> ally -> identity -> adult complicity -> hero's own secret | Use across an arc of 8-10 episodes |
| **Twist pairs** | Odd episodes: surprise twist (reader didn't know). Even episodes: suspense payoff (reader knew the plan, enjoys the bully walking into it) | Prevents twist fatigue [Hypothesis based on Hitchcock] |
| **Cost twist** | Every win costs the hero something (a friend's trust, a lie found out) | Keeps hero morally grounded, prevents "random cruelty" |
| **Plant ledger** | Every episode must plant at least one seed for a twist 2-5 episodes later and pay at least one seed from before | Keeps fairness auditable |

Pace warning: The Glory Part 1 was criticised for "the lack of revenge" and little payoff in the first eight
episodes. Short-form drama guides report revenge payoffs anywhere from "episode 3" to "teased until episode 40". For a
weekly webtoon, I recommend **a small payoff by EP2-3 at the latest** and at least a mini-sider every episode.
[Observed critique; payoff episode number is Hypothesis]

## 복선 기법 (Plant -> hide -> fire)

### 4.1 Techniques

- **Weakness camouflage**: plant the hero's tool as a flaw (always on the phone; obsessive note-taking; never talks).
- **Repetition with variation**: show the same object three times; only the third time does it matter.
- **Background plant**: put the clue in the background of a high-emotion panel, where the reader's eye is on the face.
  Note for Flow-by-hand art: the background clue must be a clearly drawable **shape** (a second phone, a red bag,
  a broken lock), never writing.
- **Dialogue plant**: a throwaway line by the bully ("Who'd believe a nobody?") becomes the hook line of the reveal.
- **False suspect**: give the reader a clear wrong suspect so the real one hides behind them (Pyramid Game EP1 uses
  the kind classmate as a twist point). [Observed]
- **Inverted Chekhov**: when designing, start from the reveal and work backward; any gun fired later must appear
  earlier. [Craft: Writing Excuses]

### 4.2 Plant tracking table (template + example for this story type)

| Plant (심기) | Where | Misdirection (숨기기) | Payoff (터뜨리기) | Gap | Fair? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero keeps phone face-down on the desk | EP1 panel ~25 | Bully mocks it: "Waiting for a call from nobody?" | EP1 end: it was recording | Same episode (~28 panels) | Fair |
| Hero is made to keep the class duty log | EP1 panel ~18 | Shown as a humiliation chore | EP3: the log dates every incident, matches the recording | 2 episodes | Fair |
| A quiet classmate looks away during the worst scene | EP1 panel ~37 | Reads as cowardice | EP2/4: they are a second witness / secret ally | 1-3 episodes | Fair if their look is drawn clearly |
| Bully's line "Nobody will believe you" | EP1 panel ~50 | Reads as a taunt | Reveal episode: played back in front of adults | Arc | Fair |
| Hero's second name / empty chair | EP1 panel ~45 (background) | Ignored | Motive twist, late arc | 8+ episodes | Fair only if re-shown once mid-arc |

Gap guideline [Hypothesis]: short plants (same episode) give the per-episode sider; medium plants (2-5 episodes)
carry the weekly twist; long plants (arc) carry the big reveal and must be **re-shown once** in between so readers
do not forget them.

## 패턴 카드 (Pattern cards for scenario team)

**Card P1 — Specific Wound**
- When: EP1, panels 4-14. Where: classroom / group chat / hallway.
- How: name the bullies and the rule they use; show through words and faces only.
- Consumer: concept-architect, tension-engineer.

**Card P2 — Weakness-Camouflaged Tool**
- When: EP1 by panel ~30; re-show once before payoff.
- How: the tool (recording, log, memory, access) is shown and mocked. Never hidden, only misread.
- Consumer: twist-master.

**Card P3 — Payoff-Promise Ending**
- When: last 5-7 panels of EP1.
- How: bully's victory lap -> silent cut -> object with new meaning -> calm count line ("Day one.") -> optional
  sting on the bully. Intent resolved, outcome open.
- Consumer: tension-engineer, concept-architect.

**Card P4 — Control Flip**
- When: final 15% of a payoff episode.
- How: stage the bully's full win -> hero's calm tell -> evidence **heard** (playback bubble / witness) in front of
  the authority -> reaction ladder ending on the bully (or withheld) -> silence.
- Consumer: twist-master.

**Card P5 — Suspense/Surprise Alternation**
- When: across episodes.
- How: in suspense episodes, show the reader the plan early (dramatic irony) and let the bully walk in; in surprise
  episodes, hide the prep and reveal at the end. Do not run two surprise-only episodes in a row. [Hypothesis]
- Consumer: series planner, tension-engineer.

**Card P6 — Domino Exposure**
- When: one per episode.
- How: each exposure reveals the next target's weak point or a new person inside the secret; end on that link.
- Consumer: series planner, twist-master.

**Card P7 — Ally/Suspect Flip**
- When: EP2-4 end, then once per arc.
- How: one clear clue on a "kind" character; confirm or reverse the next episode.
- Consumer: twist-master.

**Card P8 — Cost of Winning**
- When: after every big flip.
- How: the hero pays a small price; prevents the payoff reading as random cruelty (brief requirement).
- Consumer: concept-architect, character team.

**Card P9 — Fair-Twist Audit** (checklist before locking any script)
- Three earlier panels change meaning? Clue was shown, not hidden? Payoff uses the bully's own acts? No body violence
  as the payoff? Evidence works without readable text in the image?

## 기획 시사점 (Implications)

- EP1 should end on Card P3; the first full Card P4 control flip should arrive by EP2-3.
- Because images contain no text, every evidence reveal must be designed as **sound or speech** (playback bubbles,
  witnesses, an adult reading aloud) plus faces. Letterer and panel-director should be told early.
- Three fresh-twist options for the user (Phase 2 decision) can be built from Cards P2 + one twist type:
  (a) the hero's "loser habit" is a perfect record (control flip engine);
  (b) the hero has a hidden position inside the bully's world (proximity reveal engine);
  (c) a bully's follower is the hero's secret ally (secret-ally engine).
  These are suggestions for the synthesizer, not research findings. [Hypothesis]
