# Audience Analyst — School Revenge / Justice Webtoon for Cambodian Readers

- Observed: 2026-11-20 (fresh research for `stories/revenge`, Story 5, EP01)
- Brief: 15+ school revenge (학원 복수극), bullied student vs bullies, payoff "earned, not random cruelty";
  revenge works through evidence, timing and exposure; no gore, sex or self-harm shown; Khmer-only readers.
- Method: web search (EN/KO). Direct page fetches were blocked by this session's network for
  `searchlight.vc` and `theaseanpost.com`, so those items rely on search-result summaries (marked 2nd-hand).
- Labels: **[Data]** = sourced figure; **[Est.]** = estimate built from indirect signals;
  **[Hyp.]** = design hypothesis, untested; **[No data]** = could not be confirmed.

---

## Summary

1. **Core target [Est.]:** Cambodian readers aged ~15-24, mobile-first, reading on Facebook / phones, already
   fluent in Korean revenge content via Netflix (Squid Game, Weak Hero Class 1 charted in neighbouring SEA
   markets). Mixed gender: male readers for "weak kid outsmarts strong bullies", female readers for the
   "The Glory" style cold, planned exposure. Many have lived through or watched school bullying
   (2013 GSHS: 23.6% bullied in the past 30 days) [Data].
2. **Top immersion drivers:** (a) *justice made visible* — the bully's hidden act is exposed in front of the
   people whose opinion matters (class, teacher, parents); (b) *control* — the victim plans, the reader
   sees the trap close; (c) *underdog intelligence* over physical strength; (d) *fairness of the system*
   being restored, not just personal payback.
3. **Biggest drop risks:** too many episodes of suffering before the first payoff ("goguma" fatigue,
   "trauma porn" complaint against The Glory Part 1); revenge that works by luck/coincidence; protagonist
   becoming as cruel as the bullies; adults who are cartoonishly useless with no explanation.
4. **Cambodia-specific watch-outs:** strong teacher/parent authority and "face" mean exposure in front of
   family and school is the most powerful payoff, but a story that makes *all* teachers villains may read
   as disrespectful; informal fees / paid tutoring (rien kuo) and exam pressure are real, recognisable
   levers for plausible stakes; recent ministry focus on bullying videos and child-exploitation content
   means bullying must not be shown as fun or copyable.
5. **Design rule in one line:** first payoff in EP01 (small but real), one cheap "cider" beat every episode,
   evidence-based exposure as the main weapon, and at least one adult who finally does the right thing.

## Immersion drivers

| Driver | What hooks the reader | Trigger scenes that work | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visible justice (사이다)** | The relief after a stuck, unfair situation — "a steamed sweet potato in the throat" finally washed down | Bully's lie collapses in front of class / teacher / parents; the evidence is shown on screen | KO slang analysis; Webtoonguide review: readers "are no longer satisfied with one glass of cider" — they want frequent, escalating payoffs |
| **Control & planning** | Reader knows a trap the bully doesn't; dramatic irony | Victim quietly collects proof; a calm line before the reveal; the bully walks into the room where it ends | The Glory praise centred on the cold plan; Weak Hero praise centres on *calculated* wins |
| **Underdog intelligence** | Small/weak kid beats the strong by brain, timing, rules | Using the school's own rules, schedule, exam, CCTV, phone records against the bully | Weak Hero premise; Netflix #1 in Thailand/Malaysia for the drama adaptation |
| **System restored** | Not only "he suffered", but "the unfair system was fixed" | Corrupt adult protector loses cover; honest teacher/parent finally believes the victim | [Hyp.] strongly suggested by Cambodian data on fees/corruption + crackdown support (2014 exam crackdown had broad public support in polls) |
| **Shared recognition** | "This happened at my school" | Uniform details, morning lineup, extra paid classes, exam pressure, group chats, Facebook-posted video | [Hyp.] based on bullying prevalence and how real cases surface (victim's sister posting the video to Facebook) |
| **Emotional bond with victim** | Sympathy must be earned fast, then turned into respect | One short, quiet scene of loss; then the first decision to fight back | Pattern across revenge reviews: sympathy without agency turns into fatigue |

## Drop points (where readers leave)

| # | Drop point | Typical stage | Why they leave | Avoidance design |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Suffering marathon** | EP01-03 | Long bullying with no counter-move feels like "trauma porn"; critics said The Glory Part 1 spent so long on backstory it felt this way with little payoff | Show bullying in ≤ 30-40% of EP01 panels, through dialogue/faces/silence (as brief says); give a **first small payoff in EP01** and the first real decision to fight back by mid-episode |
| 2 | **Luck-based revenge** | Any reveal | "She just got lucky"/"too coincidental" were top complaints against The Glory | Every win must be set up by an earlier visible clue (plant → hide → fire). The protagonist *causes* the luck |
| 3 | **Protagonist becomes the bully** | Mid-arc | Readers cheer exposure, but cruelty for its own sake flips sympathy; the brief also forbids random cruelty | Revenge = truth, timing, exposure, consequences by rules. Add one moment where the protagonist *chooses not* to go too far |
| 4 | **Useless or evil adults with no reason** | EP02+ | Feels fake; for Cambodian readers, all-bad teachers can also feel disrespectful | Make adult failure specific (one teacher protected by a rich family, a principal guarding the school's face) and include at least one adult who listens once the proof exists |
| 5 | **Bully with no inner logic** | EP02-05 | One-note villains get boring; readers want the villain's fall to *mean* something | Give the main bully a pressure (parent's status, fear of losing face) that the revenge uses |
| 6 | **Payoff delayed by side plots** | Mid-arc | Romance/side lines in a revenge story were criticised as "not fitting" (The Glory love line) | Keep subplots tied to the revenge (an ally, a witness), not decoration |
| 7 | **Dragging pace / repetition** | Any | "Dropping this manhwa because it's dragging too long" type comments | One twist per episode (already a harness rule) + a cliffhanger on new information, not just new pain |
| 8 | **Too-soft ending** | Arc end | Viewers split on whether the main culprit was punished enough | Final punishment proportional and public: lose position, trust, future plans; legal/school consequence where plausible |
| 9 | **Paywall / free fatigue** | Later | One free episode raises purchase likelihood up to 13%, but beyond a threshold conversion drops; frequent paywall prompts cause fatigue | Not a direct risk if distributed free on Facebook-style channels, but keep each episode self-satisfying [Hyp.] |

## Comment reaction patterns

Direct comment scraping was not possible (Naver/Kakao comments are dynamic; Cambodian comment data
does not exist yet for this title). Patterns below are coded from reviews, forums and slang explainers.

| Type | Typical wording (paraphrased) | When it peaks | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cider cheer | "사이다!", "finally!", "that face when he realised" | Exposure panel, bully's expression after proof | Positive; the panel with the bully's face is the most quoted |
| Goguma complaint | "고구마 too long", "when does he fight back", "I can't watch this" | 2+ episodes of suffering without counter-move | Strong drop predictor |
| Plausibility complaint | "too convenient", "just got lucky", "how did nobody notice" | Big reveals that lack setup | Damages trust in the whole plan |
| Clue theory | "the phone in panel 12!", "the teacher knows something" | Episodes with visible planted clues | Strong engagement driver; plant clues readers can spot |
| Punishment debate | "not enough", "he deserved worse" vs "this is right" | Arc endings | Split; keep the main villain's fall heavier than the minions' |
| Character love/hate | Love for clever ally; hate for smug bully, enabling adult | Throughout | Hate for the bully is fuel, but needs payoff |
| Real-life testimony | "this happened at my school" | Realistic bullying scenes | Expected among Cambodian readers [Hyp.]; handle respectfully |

**Conflicting signals:** some readers want harsher payback; others leave if revenge gets cruel. Resolution:
make the *humiliation of being exposed* severe and the *physical harm* zero (fits brief and 15+).

## Cambodian reader watch-list

1. **Teacher and parent authority.** Parents generally defer to teachers ("intellectual parents"), and the
   hierarchy parent > child, teacher > student is strong; students may see speaking up as inappropriate
   [Data: cultural guides, generalised]. → The payoff that hits hardest is **exposure in front of the
   authority figures** (homeroom teacher, principal, the bully's parents). Also a reason the victim cannot
   simply "report it": the plausible obstacle is not being believed against a higher-status family.
   Show respect for the role of teacher even when one teacher is corrupt [Hyp.].
2. **Face (reputation).** Losing face publicly is a stronger, culturally readable punishment than violence.
   → Design exposure scenes around audiences (assembly, parent meeting, class chat).
3. **Real, recognisable pressures.** Informal fees to teachers and paid extra classes taught by one's own
   teacher (rien kuo) are documented; the grade 12 exam is high-stakes (2014 cheating crackdown cut the
   pass rate to ~26%). → Plausible levers: grades sold or leaked, a teacher favouring students who pay,
   exam cheating evidence. **Caution:** keep it fictional and do not portray this as universal, or as a
   comment on any real school or institution [Hyp. / sensitivity].
4. **How bullying surfaces in real life.** In a reported Phnom Penh case the school only learned of an
   assault after the victim's sister posted video on Facebook; the outcome was a signed contrition contract
   and threat of expulsion [Data, 2nd-hand, undated]. The Education Ministry (Mar 2025) condemned bullying
   after a viral video and pointed to school counsellors [Data, 2nd-hand]. → Phones, videos and Facebook
   are natural evidence tools; "a contract and a warning" is the realistic weak response that makes readers
   want a stronger, fair outcome.
5. **Sensitivity on depicting bullying.** Authorities have warned against violent content, said films create
   a "culture of violence, revenge" youth might copy, and (Aug 2026) warned creators exploiting children for
   views [Data]. → Never make bullying look cool or instructive; no step-by-step harm; the bully's
   methods are shown through aftermath and dialogue. Revenge must be legal-ish (evidence, rules, exposure),
   not vigilante violence. No self-harm or suicide depiction (brief).
6. **National/religious imagery.** Cambodia banned Kingsman 2 (2017) for depicting a temple as a villain
   hideout and the country as a crime hub [Data]. → Never use pagodas, monks or national symbols as
   villain spaces; avoid real school names (brief already says so).
7. **Gender and LGBT bullying.** LGBT students report high bullying (62%, small sample). If the story
   touches this, treat victims with dignity; avoid jokes about it [Data / sensitivity].
8. **What "plausible, satisfying justice" looks like [Hyp., synthesised]:**
   - Truth proven with evidence adults cannot ignore (video, records, witnesses).
   - Consequences through institutions: suspension/expulsion, lost scholarship or exam result, parents
     forced to apologise publicly, a corrupt teacher removed.
   - The bully loses face and status, not body.
   - The victim regains dignity and some relationship (friend, parent's trust).
   - The system is a little better at the end (others are protected).

## Planning implications (design rules)

### For male-leaning readers (underdog strategist)
- EP01: protagonist's first clever counter within the episode, even if small.
- Wins come from knowing rules/schedules/tech better than the bullies.
- No fist-fight payoff; tension through near-confrontations, threats, silence.

### For female-leaning readers (cold planned exposure)
- Long-game plan revealed piece by piece; each episode closes one trap.
- Emotion carried by faces and quiet lines; the bully's mask cracking is the key panel.

### For all Cambodian readers
- Exposure in front of teachers/parents = main cider beat; plan at least one per 2-3 episodes.
- At least one trustworthy adult appears once the evidence exists.
- Stakes rooted in exam, grades, fees and family face — fictionalised.
- Bullying shown only as needed to justify payoff, never glamorised.

### Rules for scenario team (tension-engineer, dialogue-writer, twist-master)
1. Suffering-to-payoff ratio: first counter-move by EP01 midpoint; first visible win by EP01 end.
2. Every reveal needs a clue planted earlier in the same or previous episode.
3. Each episode: one twist + one "bully's face" reaction panel + one cliffhanger on new info.
4. Protagonist refuses one cruelty per arc — keeps sympathy.
5. Main bully's fall > minions' fall; punishment public, institutional, non-physical.
6. Dialogue: short, sharp lines for exposure moments (they become comment quotes).

### Handoff notes for teammates
- **hook-analyst:** cider timing = EP01 must end with the first win or a strong "trap set" reveal;
  bully-reaction panel is the most quotable unit.
- **trend-scout:** "trauma porn" / long-suffering openers are a fatigue signal; planned-exposure revenge
  (The Glory model) still has mixed-gender appeal.
- **platform-ranker:** no Cambodian platform data available; distribution likely free / Facebook-like.
