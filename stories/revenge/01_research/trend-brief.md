## 1. One-line conclusion

Make a **15+ school revenge story where a bullied student wins by evidence, timing and public exposure**, in a
**cold, controlled, quietly furious tone**. Each episode runs **55-70 panels** and ends on a payoff-promise or
control-flip cliffhanger. The **first small win comes inside EP01**, and every episode has one twist that is planted
in advance. The bully's own words, played back in front of the people whose opinion they fear, are the main weapon.

## 3. Tone and mood

- **Emotional target:** cold control over hot rage. The reader feels the trap close before the bully does (AA
  "control and planning"; HA dramatic irony). Anger is built briefly, then turned into anticipation.
- **Texture:** quiet, tense, dialogue-led. The hero speaks little and calmly. Exposure lines are short and quotable,
  because they become the comment quotes (AA rule 6). The calm "count line" pattern ("Day one." / "Forty-one days.")
  works better than threats (HA 2.2) and keeps the story 15+.
- **Weight:** every win leaves a small aftertaste (a cost, a witness who got hurt, a choice not to go further). This
  answers the "cathartic but empty" critique of 2026's biggest school-justice hit (TS).
- **Not:** gleeful sadism, gore, bullying shown as cool or copyable (AA watch-list #5, Cambodian ministry warnings),
  all-evil adults, humiliation scenes that linger.

## 4. EP01 spine and length

**Length: 60 panels target (floor 55, ceiling 70).** This is above the harness floor of 50+ and inside the industry
band of 60-90 panels per weekly episode (PR 3.1, a secondary source, not a measured 2026 average). Dialogue-heavy:
aim for dialogue or a reaction face in most panels; silent panels are used on purpose at the turn and the cliff.
**One twist**, placed in the last ~15% of the episode.

| Beat | Panels (of 60) | What happens | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 Cold open | 1-4 | A frozen future moment: a crowd (class or parents) listening to something played aloud, one bully's face starting to crack, one line. Then the cut back ("Forty days earlier"). Plants the payoff promise in the first scroll. | PR 6.2; HA H1 |
| B2 The specific wound | 5-16 | The bullies by name and the one rule they use (a duty, a vote, a group-chat order, a "job" the hero is forced to do). Shown through words, faces, silence. No injury on screen. | HA Card P1; brief 15+ |
| B3 The system fails | 17-23 | A specific adult protects the bully for a specific reason (family status, the school's face). A "sign a letter and say sorry" outcome. At least one adult is shown as uneasy, not evil. | HA H3; AA watch-list #1, #4; D5 |
| B4 The hero's edge, disguised | 24-33 | The hero's tool is shown in plain sight and mocked as a weakness. **This is the main plant** of the episode twist. First small counter-move here (the micro-win), e.g. the hero calmly corrects a lie in front of the class or a witness hesitates. | HA H4, Card P2; PR micro-payoff; AA rule 1 |
| B5 The bottom | 34-41 | The worst humiliation of EP01, kept short and shown on faces and in silence. A quiet classmate looks away (seed for a later ally/witness). | HA H5, plant table |
| B6 The turn | 42-50 | Alone, the hero shows intent. Dialogue-light; the meaning comes from framing and one or two lines. | HA H6 |
| B7 Twist + payoff-promise cliffhanger | 51-60 | Bully's victory lap ("Who'd believe a nobody?") -> silent cut -> **the package's EP01 twist** -> a calm count line -> optional sting on the bully (a teacher asks them to stay; a voice they did not expect). Intent resolved, outcome open. | HA Card P3, 2.2; PR section 4 |

Checks for this spine: bullying share ≤ ~40% (B2+B5 ≈ 24 panels of 60); first counter-move by the midpoint; at least
three earlier panels re-read after the twist; one "bully's face" reaction panel; the cliffhanger is about **new
information**, not new pain (AA rules 1-3).

## 5. Three fresh-twist packages (user chooses one)

All three: bullied student is the hero; no bodily violence as payoff; evidence, timing and exposure; 15+; twist every
episode; the evidence can be **heard and seen on faces**, never read in the image. Character names, school, city
and Khmer terms are left to worldbuilder, character-designer and `02_story/glossary_km.md`.

### Package A: "The Cameraman" (control-flip engine)

- **Logline:** The bullies force a quiet student to film their "jokes" on their phones and his, and to delete
  everything after; he did delete everything they watched him delete, and nothing they did not see.
- **Why it is fresh:** it turns the saturated "victim humiliated on camera" setup (Viral Hit's streamer premise; the
  2026 masked-streamer trend in TS) inside out: the victim *is* the camera. The bully's own power ritual creates the
  evidence. This is mirror justice in its purest form (D2) and needs no fighting power, regression or secret
  identity. HA rates the "weakness-camouflaged tool" plus "control flip" as the strongest pair for this genre.
- **EP01 twist:** In B7, after the bullies make him wipe the phone in front of them and laugh "Nobody will believe
  you", the hero, alone, plays one clip back. We hear the ringleader's voice saying the line that matters, coming
  from a device he was never seen holding: the old phone he was mocked for in B4 (the "phone from nobody",
  face-down on his desk) was recording the room too. Calm line: "Forty-one clips. Five voices." Sting: the
  ringleader's parent is invited to school for something unrelated, and the hero is asked to film the event.
- **Series engine:** domino exposure (HA Card P6). Each arc plays one person's own words to the one audience they
  fear (the lackey to his coach, the ringleader to her parents, the protector adult to the school board). Each clip
  also reveals something new (who gave the order, who else was in the room), so every exposure opens the next target.
  Mid-series counter-twist: the bullies learn that a copy exists and hunt for it; the hero must choose whether to
  use a clip that would hurt an innocent bystander (cost beat, D6).
- **Risks:** (1) filmed bullying could read as instructive; keep every clip to sound and reaction, never show the act
  (AA #5). (2) legal-grey recording: the hero is a participant recording what is done to him, which is the safest
  case per the legal snippets in TS; let a character question it once. (3) one-tool repetition fatigue by EP06+;
  rotate the twist type (ally flip, role swap, adult complicity) while the clips stay the base. (4) a lot depends on
  audio-playback bubbles; letterer must design a clear device-tail style early.
- **Cambodian-reader fit:** strong. AA's real Phnom Penh case surfaced only when a video was posted on Facebook; a
  phone and a video are the most familiar evidence tools to a Facebook-first readership. The payoff (losing face in
  front of parents and teachers) fits AA watch-list #1-2. Needs care that the story never looks like a guide to
  filming or posting minors (Aug 2026 ministry warning in AA).

### Package B: "The Tutor" (proximity-reveal engine)

- **Logline:** The bullied scholarship student is hired, by a mother who has no idea, as the private tutor of the
  ringleader's younger sibling, and every evening he sits at the bully's family table.
- **Why it is fresh:** it moves revenge from the school into the bully's home, where their status really lives (the
  parents' trust and family face). Fresh against fists, regression and masked heroes. **Not fully fresh:** The Glory's
  Part 1 hook was the heroine becoming the teacher of the bully's daughter (HA 1.1), so readers who know The Glory
  may see the debt. The difference must come from the sibling becoming a real ally and from the tutoring economy.
- **EP01 twist:** B7 cuts from the ringleader's school victory lap to the ringleader's front door that evening. The
  mother introduces "your little sister's new tutor". The ringleader must greet him politely in front of her parent
  (HA "hidden position" amplifier: the bully's forced courtesy). Calm line from the hero: "Same time tomorrow."
- **Series engine:** suspense episodes (the reader knows he is inside; the bully must perform the good child at home)
  alternate with surprise episodes (what he learned at the table is used at school). The sibling slowly sees the
  truth and becomes the key witness (secret-ally engine). The protector adult is a parent, which matches TS "adults as
  targets" and AA "system restored".
- **Risks:** (1) a child in the middle of a revenge plan: the hero must **never** use or endanger the sibling; this
  must be his line he refuses to cross (D6), or the story reads as cruel. (2) The Glory comparison. (3) home scenes
  add a second set (more Flow work). (4) why would a rich family hire him? needs a plausible reason (top exam score,
  a teacher's recommendation).
- **Cambodian-reader fit:** strong recognition but sensitive. Paid extra tutoring (rien kuo) and exam pressure are
  real and recognisable (AA watch-list #3), and family face is the strongest stake. But tutoring fees and teacher
  favouritism must stay fictional and never be framed as a comment on Cambodian schools in general (AA caution).

### Package C: "The Wrong Suspect" (false-suspect + secret-ally engine)

- **Logline:** When a voice starts leaking the bullies' secrets, they blame the boy they bully; he did not do it, but
  to survive he must find the real leaker first, and it is the ringleader's best friend.
- **Why it is fresh:** it bends the "anonymous exposer" hybrid that TS rates strong (hidden identity x exposure) by
  making the hero the *framed* suspect, not the hidden avenger, then pairing him with a secret ally from inside the bully
  circle (HA Card P7 and "secret ally" twist; the Pyramid Game ally/suspect flip). Two unlikely partners, with
  trust as the constant tension.
- **EP01 twist:** The bullies corner the hero over the leaks (B5). In B7 a new leak lands while he was, provably,
  sitting with a teacher (alibi planted in B3-B4). The ringleader's best friend, the follower who looked away in B5,
  stops him at the gate and says quietly: "It was me. And I can't finish it alone."
- **Series engine:** the pair's alliance plus each episode's question "can I trust you?". Ally/suspect flips are built
  in; each leak exposes one secret and pulls the hunt closer to them. Arc end: role swap (the follower started part of
  the bullying).
- **Risks:** (1) **highest production risk under the no-text rule**: leaks, accounts and posts are text by nature;
  every leak must become a voice note, a speech, a phone call or a person reading it aloud, or the story goes blank on
  screen. (2) online exposure can read as doxxing as heroism (TS caution). (3) the hero is reactive in EP01 (framed,
  not acting), which works against D3; he needs his own counter-move by the midpoint. (4) two leads split the focus.
- **Cambodian-reader fit:** medium. The anonymous-account frame is very online (Facebook-first readers will get it),
  but its payoff is weaker on "face before parents and teachers" unless every leak ends in a physical, in-person
  confrontation. A friendship across the social divide is an easy emotional hook.

**Recommendation: Package A, "The Cameraman".** It is the only option where the research's strongest signals all
point the same way: the less crowded non-violent lane (D1), mirror justice (D2), a hero with agency in EP01 (D3), a
planted control flip (HA's top mechanism), the phone-video evidence culture Cambodian readers know (AA), and the
lowest risk under the no-text rule. Package B is the best alternative if the user wants a stronger family drama; it
can also be folded into A later (for example, an arc where the hero must film an event at the ringleader's home).
**The user decides.**

## 6. Hook and twist strategy (applies to any package)

- **Adopted pattern cards (HA):** P1 Specific wound (EP01 B2), P2 Weakness-camouflaged tool (EP01 B4, re-shown once
  before payoff), P3 Payoff-promise ending (EP01 B7), P4 Control flip (first full one by EP02-03), P5
  Suspense/surprise alternation (series), P6 Domino exposure (series engine), P7 Ally/suspect flip (EP02-04, then once
  per arc), P8 Cost of winning (after every big flip), P9 Fair-twist audit (before any script is locked).
- **Twist ladder per arc:** object -> ally -> hidden position -> adult complicity -> the hero's own secret (HA 3.3).
- **Plant ledger:** each episode plants at least one seed for 2-5 episodes later and pays one older seed; long plants
  are re-shown once mid-arc (HA 4.2).
- **Reaction ladder at every exposure:** crowd -> follower -> bully -> adult; the bully's face last, then a silent
  panel (HA 3.2). AA: that face panel is the most quoted unit.

## 7. Target reader and drop avoidance

- **Core reader [Provisional]:** Cambodian students of grade 9-12 plus readers aged up to ~24, on phones, often
  through Facebook-style free channels, used to Korean revenge drama (AA; estimate, no platform data). Estimated
  ~55/45 male/female: "weak kid outsmarts the strong" pulls male readers, "cold planned exposure" pulls female readers.
- **Drop-avoidance rules (from AA drop table, checked by HA and PR):**
  1. Bullying ≤ 30-40% of EP01; counter-move by the midpoint; small win by the end.
  2. No luck: every win has a visible earlier plant.
  3. The hero refuses one cruelty per arc.
  4. Adult failure is specific and motivated; one adult does the right thing once proof exists.
  5. The main bully has an inner logic and a pressure point.
  6. Subplots serve the revenge (ally, witness), not decoration.
  7. Every cliffhanger is new information, not just new pain; never two threat endings in a row.
  8. Final punishment is public, institutional and proportional: loss of position, trust, a scholarship or exam
     place, a forced public apology; the ringleader falls harder than the followers.
- **Paywall-friendly structure:** every episode ending works as a "pay to read the next one early" pitch (Naver
  preview logic); if a free/paid boundary is ever set, put it on the episode just before the first big exposure;
  each paid episode gives at least one partial payoff (PR section 5). For free Facebook-style distribution this matters
  less, but each episode still needs to satisfy on its own (AA #9).

## 8. Production constraints (Khmer edition, Flow-by-hand)

These follow from CLAUDE.md and the `webtoon-khmer-edition` / `webtoon-flow-handoff` skills, and they change how
evidence works in this genre.

1. **No text in any image.** Phones, screens, notes, letters, posters, group chats and documents are drawn **blank**.
   The reader can never read evidence. So **evidence must be carried by speech and faces**:
   - **Audio playback** in a speech bubble with a distinct "device" tail (from a phone or speaker) carrying the
     bully's own words. The letterer must define this bubble style before scripting EP01 (HA 3.2).
   - **A witness or adult says it aloud** ("This message says...", "You said, on the 14th...").
   - **Dates, counts and names are spoken**, never written ("Forty-one clips", "five names").
   - **Reaction faces** carry the content: the crowd, then the follower, then the bully, then the adult.
2. **Clues must be drawable shapes**, never writing: an old phone face-down, a red phone case, a cracked screen,
   a second chair, a seat by the window, a gate, a specific bag. Re-use the same shape three times so readers can
   spot it (HA 4.1).
3. **Every panel keeps the empty area** named in the lettering spec's `art_space` for the Khmer bubble. Dialogue-heavy
   panels need room; plan wider establishing shots for multi-speaker exposure scenes.
4. **Script:** every dialogue line gets a natural Khmer `KM:` line; names and terms come only from
   `02_story/glossary_km.md`. Exposure lines must be short so the Khmer stays readable in one bubble.
5. **Viewer:** built only with `km_tool.py build`; `km_tool.py check --episode 01` before building and release.
6. **Images:** made by hand by the user in Google Flow. Phase 4 writes prompts, builds the handoff page and stops.
   Fewer sets = less Flow work; Package A and C stay mostly in school, Package B adds a home set.
7. **15+ depiction rules:** bullying through dialogue, faces, silence and aftermath; no injuries shown; no self-harm
   or suicide; no step-by-step harm; no filming of the abusive act itself in panels (show the person holding the
   phone and the faces, not the act). No pagodas, monks or national symbols in villain spaces; no real school or
   person (AA watch-list #5-6, brief).

## Summary

1. Lane: 15+ school revenge won by evidence, timing and public exposure, not fists; this lane is less crowded than the fight-led hits and avoids the 2026 "cathartic but empty" backlash.
2. Tone: cold, quiet control; short quotable exposure lines; every win planted, proportional, and with a small cost.
3. EP01: 60 panels (55-70), cold open, specific wound, failed system, disguised tool, bottom, turn, then one planted twist and a payoff-promise cliffhanger; bullying ≤ 40%, a small win inside EP01.
4. User chooses: A "The Cameraman" (recommended), B "The Tutor", or C "The Wrong Suspect"; with no text in images, evidence is heard through playback bubbles and spoken lines and shown on faces.
5. Evidence is snippet-level: no live rankings, no Cambodian reader data, estimated arc timing; re-check before locking the series plan.
