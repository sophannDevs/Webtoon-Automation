## 1. The fair-play rule (binding on every episode)

A twist may be printed only if it passes all six checks. script-editor and quality-reviewer run this table per episode.

| # | Rule | What it means on the page |
|---|---|---|
| FP1 | **Three plants minimum.** | Every twist has **at least three plant panels before the reveal**. For EP02+ at least one plant is in an **earlier episode** and at least one is in the **same episode**. Plant locations are listed per episode in §4 (EP01: exact panels in §3). |
| FP2 | **Shown, not hidden.** | Each plant is fully on the page: a visible object or position, a bubble the reader reads, a sound word, or a face. No plant is off-panel, cut away from, or told only after the reveal. The reveal **re-frames**; it never adds a fact the reader could not have had. |
| FP3 | **Misread, not withheld.** | The plant is weakened only by **where the reader is looking**: a louder misdirection beside it (named per twist as "Misdirection"), a joke, a character's dismissal, or an ordinary reading of a double-meaning line. Lying narration is not allowed (there is no narrator; only captions for time cuts). |
| FP4 | **Text-free.** | A plant may not be a written word, number, screen, label or caption in the art. Visual plants are objects, positions, colours (olive = the archive) and faces; audio plants are speech and sound-word bubbles drawn by the viewer. |
| FP5 | **Khmer-true.** | A plant that rests on words must work in the Khmer line, not only in English (e.g. the shared root ចប់ in "It's finished" / "Finish before the bell", the identical "បិទទ្វារទៅ។"). Matching lines across episodes use **identical Khmer**, letter for letter. |
| FP6 | **Rule-true and costed.** | The reveal obeys world.md R1-R5, D7-D14 and characters.md motives, and leaves a **cost or dilemma** that powers the next episode (column "Cost / engine"). A twist with no cost is cut. |

Visal sometimes knows more than the reader (Surprise episodes). That is fair only if the reader saw every plant that
Visal used. He never wins with a fact the reader never saw.
