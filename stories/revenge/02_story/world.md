## 0. World overview

**Tonle Meas** is a fictional mid-sized riverside provincial town in a Cambodian-friendly world: one long market road,
motos and tuk-tuks, iced-coffee carts, dust in the dry season, a brown river at the edge of town. In its newer
quarter, **Sangkat Chamkar Svay** (an old mango-orchard district now half houses, half building-material yards), stands
**Twin Tamarind High School**, a public lower-and-upper secondary school of about 1,400 students, named for the two old
tamarind trees in the middle of its yard.

Twin Tamarind looks like a good school: a new donated meeting hall, a strong volleyball team, a good honour roll.
Under that face it runs on three currencies: **face** (what parents and the town see), **money** (donations, extra-class
fees) and **silence** (what students and teachers agree not to say). The Ringleader's family supplies the money, the
school sells the face, and the Hero has been paying in silence.

The Hero's place in this world is a **job**: at the midday break, in the emptied Grade 11 classroom, he films the
Ringleader's group's "jokes" on the Lackey's phone, then deletes the clip in front of them before the afternoon bell.
The world's central rule, the one the bullies made themselves, is **"Nothing lives past the bell."** The story is the
discovery that something did.

All places, names and institutions are fictional. No real school, town, person or case. The school is **not** on pagoda
grounds; no pagoda, monk or national symbol appears in any scene where bullying, cover-up or villainy happens.

## 3. How the school works: the five core rules (with their costs)

These are the rules of the **school world**. Each makes conflict and each has a cost.

### R1. Face is the currency. (Root rule)
What parents, teachers and the town *see* matters more than what *happened*. A private complaint can be absorbed; a
public loss of face cannot.
- **What it makes:** adults bury problems quietly ("sign a letter and say sorry"), and the only place a truth cannot be
  buried is **in front of the audience whose opinion holds someone's status**. This is why the Hero plays clips in rooms,
  never online (concept §5.1): the room is where face lives.
- **Cost:** face works both ways. Any public play also costs face to the innocent people in the clip, to the Hero (he
  was the Cameraman) and to his family. Every exposure scene must show one face that did not deserve to fall (D6).

### R2. The midday hour belongs to whoever stays.
Classes run **07:00-11:00** and **14:00-17:00**. In the long midday break almost everyone leaves: home for lunch, to the
gate stalls, to extra classes in town. Block B 2F is empty and the duty teacher sits downstairs in the Staff Office fan.
- **What it makes:** a private stage in a public building. At midday 11C becomes the Ringleader's room: louvres shut,
  door half closed, the Lackey at the back door. The job happens here, and the old phone has been lying on the
  Hero's desk since morning.
- **Cost:** what happens in the empty hour has no witness, so a student's word is worthless. And the hour is not quite
  empty: the Staff Office is right below and adults walk the stairs (§8, the fifth voice).

### R3. "Nothing lives past the bell." (The bullies' rule)
The Ringleader's group films its "jokes" on **the Lackey's phone**, held by the Hero. They watch it once, laugh, and at
the 13:55 warning strike of the bell the Hero must **delete it in front of them**, including from the phone's deleted
folder, while the Ringleader watches the screen (screen drawn plain glowing; we see faces and his thumb, never the
screen content). Then the Lackey takes his phone back.
- **What it makes:** the Ringleader's caution (her parent must never see anything) creates the ritual, and the ritual
  creates a witness she never counted: the Hero, and the object beside him.
- **Cost (to them):** the rule makes them feel safe, so they talk freely during the job. **Cost (to the Hero):** he is
  ordered to say the start word ("Rolling.") on every clip. His own voice is on all forty-one recordings, as the one
  who held the camera. Any clip he plays also proves he took part (see T-H4).

### R4. Phones go in the bag; a phone in a teacher's hand goes in the drawer.
School rule: phones are banned on desks during lessons. A teacher who sees one confiscates it to the **padlocked drawer
in the Staff Office** until a parent collects it.
- **What it makes:** the old phone survives *because* it is beneath notice. Teachers ignore it on his desk because it
  looks dead ("Is that even a phone?"); the bullies ignore it because it is a joke. Being mocked is its armour.
- **Cost:** the day anyone takes it seriously, one teacher's hand ends the archive (a confiscation is the cleanest
  threat beat in the hunt; it also puts the phone inside the adults' room).

### R5. Money buys rooms.
Some doors at Twin Tamarind open with money: the homeroom teacher's paid evening **extra class** (where the monthly
exam's shape is "hinted"), the **School Support Committee** seat that comes with a donation, the **New Hall** that
carries a donor family's reputation, the Coach's provincial-selection list.
- **What it makes:** the Ringleader's family is inside every room the Hero is locked out of. The Protector Adult owes
  that family. The Lackey owes the Coach.
- **Cost:** the same rooms are where face is lost. Money gets the Ringleader's parent a front-row seat at the parents'
  event, and that seat is exactly where the cold open freezes.

**Priority if rules collide:** R1 > R3 > R2 > R4 > R5 (face decides everything; the bullies' own ritual is the next
strongest force).

## 4. School systems (what the story uses)

### 4-1. Discipline ladder
| Step | What happens | Who decides | Story use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Warning | A teacher's word in the corridor | Any teacher | Ignored by the strong |
| 2 Conduct book | The student's name written in the class conduct book (drawn as a closed plain book) | Homeroom teacher | Spoken: "Third time this month." |
| 3 Apology letter | Both students sign a **letter of apology** in the Deputy's room, shake hands, "the matter is closed" | Deputy Principal | **EP01 B3 "the system fails"**: the Hero is made to sign too, as if both were at fault |
| 4 Parents summoned | Parents called to the Staff Office | Deputy Principal | A threat both ways: the Ringleader fears it most |
| 5 Suspension / transfer | Out for days or moved to another school | Principal + committee | Rarely used on donors' children; used freely on the poor |

- The apology letter is the world's symbol of a buried case. Drawn as **a single sheet with blank lines and two
  signature spaces**, never readable. Its spoken name is the joke the bullies repeat ("You signed. It's closed.").

### 4-2. Exams and rank
- **Monthly exams** in each subject; results read out by rank in class (spoken only). **Semester exams** in March and
  August. **Grade 12 national exam** at the end of next year: this is the stake for a Grade 11 student (a ruined record
  now follows you there).
- **Honour roll:** the top students of each grade are named at the parents' event. The Ringleader is on it every year;
  her parent expects it. A slip in rank is already a loss of face.
- The Hero's rank is quietly good and unrewarded (he cannot pay for extra class, so he is not "one of the teacher's").

### 4-3. Extra classes and informal fees (kept specific to this school, never a statement about all schools)
- The homeroom teacher of 11C runs a paid **evening extra class** in a rented room in town. Students who attend hear what
  the monthly exam "might look like". Paying is "voluntary"; not paying is noticed.
- A small **handout fee** is collected in class for photocopies; the Hero sometimes cannot pay and stands to say so
  (a small, recognisable humiliation; keep brief).
- The Ringleader's parent pays for the whole extra class's fan and lights, and is thanked for it in front of others.
- Rule for writers: fees are shown through **one adult's habit and other adults' unease**, never framed as how Cambodian
  schools work. At least one teacher refuses the system (the Good Adult does not run extra classes).

### 4-4. The parents' event (Day 40, the cold open)
- **Parents' Meeting and Honour Ceremony**, held once a semester in the New Hall: speeches, the honour roll, thanks to
  donors, then homeroom teachers meet parents. Parents sit in rows; teachers in front; the donor family in the front row.
- The school has **no paid photographer**. A teacher picks "a student with a steady hand" to film the ceremony from the
  **AV table at the back of the hall, next to the sound mixer and the two big speakers**. In EP01's sting the Good Adult
  (or another teacher) picks the Hero, because the bullies' nickname has spread: "You're the Cameraman, aren't you?"
- The AV table is the plant for the cold open: the Hero sits where sound goes into the room. How a clip reaches the
  speakers is **never shown step by step** (§5, D-rule 10); we see the table, his hand, the speakers, the faces.

### 4-5. Who has power (ladder of who can silence whom)
1. **The Ringleader's Parent**: donor of the New Hall, seat on the School Support Committee, a building-materials
   business that supplies half the town. Never seen threatening anyone; does not need to.
2. **The Principal**: wants the school's face and the next donation; mostly away at meetings (drawn rarely).
3. **The Protector Adult** (institutional slot: the Deputy Principal for student affairs, owner of the discipline
   ladder; character-designer may put another adult in the slot if the reason stays specific): keeps cases quiet for the
   school's face and for the donor family.
4. **The Coach**: decides provincial-selection places; the Lackey's whole future.
5. **The homeroom teacher of 11C**: rank, conduct book, extra class.
6. **The Ringleader**: power borrowed from her parent, used with care because she fears that parent.
7. **The Lackey, Follower 1, Follower 2**: power borrowed from the Ringleader.
8. **Everyone else**: power of silence. The Quiet Classmate is here.
9. **The Hero**: none on paper. In practice, the only person who has heard everyone.

## 5. The old phone: device rules

The weapon has to be **limited, drawable and never instructive**. concept.md §5's six rules are copied first; D7-D14 are
the device's physical rules that serve them.

### 5-1. The six fixed evidence rules (from concept.md §5, unchanged)
- **E1 He never posts.** No upload, no group chat, no Facebook. He plays recordings **in the room**, face to face, to a
  specific audience.
- **E2 He only recorded what was done to him, or in front of him.** A participant, not a spy. Once in Arc 1 someone
  (the Good Adult or the Quiet Classmate) asks whether recording was right; the question stands, no real law is cited.
- **E3 Sound first.** The archive is mostly audio. On the page, evidence = a voice from a device (playback bubble with a
  device tail) + the faces hearing it. The act itself is never shown, filmed or replayed as an image.
- **E4 No hands.** He never touches anyone. Payoffs are institutional and proportional; the Ringleader falls hardest.
- **E5 Every play is planted.** At least three earlier panels change meaning when it fires.
- **E6 Short, quotable lines.** Count lines replace threats ("Forty-one clips." / "Day twelve.").

### 5-2. What it looks like (signature prop, same in every panel)
- A **thick, old, budget smartphone** about ten years out of date (fictional, no brand, no logo): chunky body, rounded
  corners, small screen with a wide bezel, **one small round camera lens at the top of the back**.
- **Back:** dull **olive-green** plastic case, worn shiny at the edges, a **diagonal crack across the bottom-right
  corner of the case**, and a strip of yellowed clear tape over that corner holding the back cover on. One small faded
  **orange round sticker** (no print) near the lens.
- **Front (seen rarely):** the screen has a **spiderweb crack in the same bottom-right corner**. Screen is dark, or a
  plain dim glow. Never readable, never an interface.
- **Always face-down** on the top-left corner of his desk (§2-2), lens side up. When he carries it, it is in his left
  shirt pocket, top edge just visible.
- **Companion prop:** **white wired earphones** with the left bud wrapped in black tape. He listens at night with one ear.
- Its nickname among the bullies: **"the Brick"** (KM proposal ឥដ្ឋ). Character-designer fixes its size against hands.

### 5-3. Physical rules (limits = tension)
- **D7 Audio, not video.** Face-down, the lens sees only the desk. So the archive is voices, room sound, the bell, the
  fan. No image of any act exists anywhere in the story (matches E3 and rating §9.4).
- **D8 It hears only what is near it.** **Desk mode** (most clips): it lies on his desk; it catches 11C clearly, the
  corridor faintly, the Staff Office below not at all. **Pocket mode** (rare): in his shirt pocket the sound is muffled,
  rustling, half-lost; voices identifiable, some words gone. A pocket clip can prove *who* spoke but not always *every*
  word. (Twist-master may use a lost word as a gap.)
- **D9 The battery is weak.** A full charge lasts about one school day **only if the screen stays dark**. On hot days it
  dies in the afternoon session. Anything said after it dies is lost. It charges overnight at home from the one wall
  socket the household shares (the cost of power is real for his family). A dead battery is a hole in the record and a
  dramatic clock ("It's at the last bar.").
- **D10 The storage is small.** A small memory card that is **nearly full at forty-one clips**. To keep recording he
  must eventually delete his own old clips. Each new recording costs an old one: the archive is a budget, not a vault.
- **D11 No internet.** No data plan, no working SIM. It cannot join the class group chat, cannot upload, cannot be
  reached. That is why it is mocked ("a phone from nobody") and why E1 is physically true in Arc 1: there is nothing to
  post *with*.
- **D12 One archive, no copy (at EP01).** The forty-one clips live only on that card. Lose the phone or the card, lose
  everything. Making a copy needs someone else's device or computer, which means trusting someone: a decision for
  series-plotter (the copy hunt, concept §4 gear 3).
- **D13 Each clip surprises once.** A played clip is still on the card, but its target now knows it exists; it has no
  second ambush. The Hero counts **unplayed** clips: "Forty-one." -> later "Thirty-eight." The count only goes down.
- **D14 No how-to.** How he starts, stops, selects or plays a recording is **never shown** beyond one thumb on the side
  of the phone. No menus, no screens, no settings, no hiding technique (rating §9.5).

### 5-4. The count: "Forty-one clips. Five voices."
- A **clip** = one midday job (or one pocket-mode scene) recorded from start word to bell.
- A **voice** = a person heard on the archive **saying the words that did harm**: ordering, mocking, threatening, or
  **permitting** it. Bystanders who only speak otherwise are not counted (so the Quiet Classmate is on clips but is not a
  "voice", unless twist-master later decides otherwise and says so).
- The four visible "voices": the Ringleader, the Lackey, Follower 1, Follower 2. **The fifth is the well (§8-1).**
- The Hero's own voice ("Rolling.") is on every clip and is **not** counted. That he does not count himself is itself a
  seed (§8-5).
- The archive runs from **the second semester of last year (Grade 10)** to EP01. The job began then. So the forty-one
  clips cover about five months, not forty days.

### 5-5. Why the bullies never suspected it
1. It looks dead (screen always dark, cracked, taped).
2. It has no internet, so in their world it cannot *do* anything.
3. They watch him delete; they trust what they see (R3).
4. It is the joke: every time they mock it they confirm to themselves it is harmless. (Plant shown 3 times in EP01 B4.)

## 7. Taboos and costs

### 7-1. School taboos (the social world)
| # | Taboo | Who enforces | Result of breaking it |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-S1 | A student does not accuse a donor's child. | Protector Adult, homeroom teacher | The accuser signs the apology letter too; parents summoned; whispers of transfer. (EP01 B3) |
| T-S2 | A teacher does not contradict the donor family. | Principal, Protector Adult | Extra classes taken away, duties changed, hints of a far-away posting. (The Good Adult's risk) |
| T-S3 | Family business does not come to school; school trouble does not go home. | Everyone | The Ringleader's whole caution. When school trouble reaches her parent in public, her status collapses. |
| T-S4 | What is "closed" stays closed. | The apology letter | Re-opening a signed case looks like disrespect to the adults who closed it: the Hero needs proof the room cannot refuse. |
| T-S5 | Nobody speaks about the midday hour. | The silent middle | Whoever speaks is next; the Quiet Classmate's silence has this cause. |

### 7-2. The Hero's own taboos (the line between justice and becoming the bully)
| # | Taboo | Cost if he breaks it |
|---|---|---|
| T-H1 | Never post, never send, never share outside the room. (E1) | He becomes what the August-warning world fears; the story loses its moral line; the clip escapes his control and hurts bystanders. |
| T-H2 | Never touch anyone. (E4) | Any violence makes him "the one who started it" under R1 and ends his credibility instantly. |
| T-H3 | Never play a clip that shames an innocent bystander without facing that cost. | At least once per arc he refuses to play one (D6). If he plays it anyway, he loses an ally (the bystander) for good. |
| T-H4 | Never pretend he was not there. | Every clip carries his own "Rolling." Playing a clip exposes him as the Cameraman too; he must accept that cost each time. |
| T-H5 | Never spend the last clip on anger. | The archive is finite (D10, D13). A wasted clip is a target who walks free. |

### 7-3. Production taboos (binding, from brief and concept §9)
- No injuries, blood or blows on screen; bullying = words, faces, silence, the Hero holding a phone, aftermath (a wet bag,
  a missing chair, a closed door).
- **The act is never in a panel.** At the job, frame the Hero holding the Lackey's phone, the watchers' faces, the shut
  louvres, the door. Never what the lens sees.
- No sexual content; no self-harm or suicide in any form; insults short and non-graphic; threats implied.
- No how-to for recording, hiding or posting (D14).
- **No pagodas, monks, or national symbols** (flag, Angkor imagery, royal motifs) in any villain, cover-up or bullying
  scene. The flagpole stays bare or off-frame. The New Hall decor is neutral (paper flowers, plain cloth).
- No real school, town, person or case.

## 8. Mystery wells (deliberate blanks for twist-master and series-plotter)

### 8-1. The fifth voice (main well, pays at the Arc 1 / Arc 2 turn)
- **What the reader knows in EP01:** "Five voices." The group visibly has four. Readers will guess.
- **Hard constraints (any answer must obey them):**
  1. The fifth voice was **within earshot of the Hero's desk or pocket** (D8). If desk mode: in 11C or at its door at
     midday. If pocket mode: in a room the Hero was made to stand in (e.g. the Deputy's room at the apology letter).
  2. The voice says words that **ordered or permitted** the harm (§5-4 definition), not merely heard it.
  3. It must be **audible but unidentified** in at least one earlier clip played on the page (fair-play plant: an adult
     tone, a phrase, a sound such as keys or the Staff Office fan through the door).
  4. It cannot be resolved by luck; the Hero has known since before EP01.
- **Candidates (twist-master chooses; worldbuilder's ranking):**
  - **(a) Recommended: the Protector Adult** at the 11C door at midday, finding the job in progress and saying something
    like "Keep it quiet and close the door. Finish before the bell." Pays concept §7 Arc 2 directly.
  - **(b) The homeroom teacher**, whose extra-class income depends on the donor family; heard in pocket mode at the
    apology letter telling the Hero "Sign it. Don't make trouble for her family."
  - **(c) The Ringleader's Parent** on speaker-phone during a job ("Whatever it is, finish it before it reaches me."):
    strongest shock, but makes the parent too dark for D5; use only if twist-master rebalances.
- Rejected: the Good Adult (it would break D5's "one good adult"), the Quiet Classmate (not an orderer; keep this character for
  §8-3).

### 8-2. The gaps in the record
- Battery (D9) and pocket mode (D8) leave holes. **One clip in the archive stops mid-sentence**: the battery died on a
  hot day. What was said after it? Someone in the story knows. A well for the copy-hunt phase.

### 8-3. Who else was in the room
- The Quiet Classmate is audible on several clips (a chair, a breath, once a whispered word). Why the Quiet Classmate stayed at midday
  when everyone else left is **not explained** in Arc 1. A seed for the ally flip (P7) and light bond (D10).

### 8-4. Where the phone came from
- The old phone is a hand-me-down. **Whose** it was (and why the Hero keeps such an object) is left blank for
  character-designer. It must not be a supernatural or "secret tech" object; its power is only that nobody looks at it.

### 8-5. The Hero's own clip (season-end well, concept §7)
- The Hero never counts his own voice. Somewhere in the archive (or from before the job began) is a recording of **his own
  silence** when someone else was the target. It is never played until the season end. World support: the archive
  started in Grade 10 semester 2, and the Hero was "the one holding the phone" before he was the one being mocked.
  Twist-master defines the content within the rating rules.

### 8-6. Season 2 option (not committed)
- Someone else at Twin Tamarind has been recording too (concept §7). The world allows it: R2's empty hour and R4's
  confiscation drawer both imply other phones, other archives.

## 10. Visual tone (for art-director)

- **Overall:** realistic, clean Korean-webtoon school drama rendering, Cambodian-friendly setting. Cold, quiet, tense.
  Contrast **hot exterior light** with **cool, controlled interiors** around the Hero.
- **Palette:**
  - School: pale ochre-yellow walls, faded teal louvres/doors/railings, red-brown roof tile, pale dust-coloured yard,
    the deep green of the twin tamarinds.
  - Uniforms: **white short-sleeved shirt**; boys **navy trousers**, girls **navy knee-length pleated skirt**; a small
    **plain teal round badge** on the shirt pocket (no lettering); black shoes or sandals. PE: teal T-shirt, navy shorts
    (Lackey on court).
  - The Ringleader's colour signal: a **pale pearl-pink phone case** and a neat pale-pink hair clip (character-designer
    confirms). The Lackey's phone (the job phone): **large, matte black case**.
  - **The Hero's colour signal: the olive-green of the old phone**, echoed nowhere else on him. Readers learn "olive =
    the archive".
  - The New Hall: cream and gold, soft red flowers: warm, proud, about to crack.
  - Playback moments: a **cool desaturated blue-grey cast** on the listeners' faces when a recorded voice plays (art
    language for "the past is speaking"). The device-tail bubble itself is the letterer's.
- **Light grammar:** the job = hard noon stripes through shut louvres (bars, a cage); exposure = even, bright, public
  light with nowhere to hide; the Hero's turn = one bulb, deep shadow, the phone's back catching light.
- **Architecture:** low two-storey concrete blocks with open corridors, louvre windows, ceiling fans, bare tubes, a
  zinc-roofed moto shed, a green iron gate. Practical, sun-worn, not poor-porn; the New Hall is the only shiny building.
- **Weather:** dry season throughout Arc 1: blue sky, dust, haze in the morning, no rain. (Rain is saved; if series-plotter
  wants a weather turn, the first rain can mark Arc 2.)
- **Sound-in-image cues (no text):** the bell = the wheel rim mid-swing with the steel bar; the fan = blades blurred;
  a voice from the phone = the phone face-up for once, the listeners turned toward it.
- **Text-free rule:** every blank surface listed in §2-3. Calm areas for bubbles: walls above the louvres, the sky over
  the yard, the hall's high ceiling, the dark side of the Hero's room.

## Summary

1. Setting: fictional riverside town Tonle Meas, Sangkat Chamkar Svay, **Twin Tamarind High School** (Grade 11, class 11C); runs on face, money and silence; no real places, no pagodas/monks/national symbols near villainy.
2. Places: 12 text-free LOC_* tokens (classroom + midday mode, corridor, yard with twin tamarinds and the wheel-rim bell, staff office, deputy's room, New Hall with AV table, volleyball court, gate, moto shed, storeroom, the Hero's room) with fixed screen directions and the phone's fixed desk spot.
3. School rules: R1 face is currency, R2 the midday hour belongs to whoever stays, R3 "Nothing lives past the bell" (supervised wipe; the Hero must say "Rolling."), R4 phones get confiscated, R5 money buys rooms; plus the discipline ladder (apology letter), exams/honour roll, extra-class fees and the Day 40 parents' event where the Hero films from the AV table.
4. The old phone: olive, cracked bottom-right corner, taped, always face-down; audio only, hears only what is near, weak battery, nearly full storage, no internet, one archive, each clip surprises once, no how-to; concept's six evidence rules kept verbatim; "41 clips, 5 voices" defined (unplayed count, voice = said or permitted harm).
5. Fuel: factions and power ladder, school and Hero taboos with costs, mystery wells (fifth voice with hard constraints, recommended answer the Protector Adult; a cut-off clip; the Quiet Classmate's midday; the phone's origin; the Hero's own clip), a 40-day text-free clock (tamarind pods, hall prep), and a hot-light-outside / cold-control-inside visual tone.
