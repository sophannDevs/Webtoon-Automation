| Item | Proposal |
|---|---|

| Khmer title (proposal) | **ចាក់ឡើងវិញ** ("play it again / replay", 2 words). Alternative: **អ្នកថត** ("the one who films", the bullies' nickname for the Hero). See §14 note for the glossary. |

Why: the whole series is one act repeated: the bullies' own words, **played back** to the one audience they fear. The
title is a command the Hero never says aloud until the ringleader's fall. It has no wordplay, so it carries straight
into Khmer, and it is short enough for a Facebook post header and the viewer title bar.

## 2. High concept, logline, pitch

- **High concept (1 line):** They made the quiet kid film their "jokes" and delete every clip in front of them;
  the old phone face-down on his desk never stopped recording.
- **Logline (1 line):** A bullied student forced to be his tormentors' cameraman (a flawed, silent hero who has
  stopped expecting any adult to help) secretly keeps every recording they thought he deleted, and plays each bully's
  own words back to the one audience they fear, until the clips start pointing at an adult who should have stopped it,
  and at a silence of his own.
- **3-line pitch:**
  1. At school his job is fixed: hold the phone, film what the Ringleader's group calls a joke, then wipe it while
     they watch. "Nobody will believe a nobody."
  2. He deleted everything they watched him delete. The cracked old phone they mock, lying face-down on his desk,
     heard everything they did not see.
  3. He never posts and never hits. He waits for the right room (a coach's office, a parents' meeting, a board
     hearing) and presses play.

## 3. 70% familiar + one fresh twist

**Familiar (the genre codes readers already love, kept as they are):**
- A bullied student, a school social ladder, a ringleader protected by family status, followers who obey.
- Adults who look away for a reason; a "sign a letter and say sorry" non-outcome.
- Cold, planned revenge by exposure (The Glory lane), underdog intelligence instead of strength (Weak Hero's spirit).
- Mirror justice and the bully's face falling in public: the 사이다 payoff.

**Fresh (exactly one pillar flipped): the victim *is* the camera.**
The saturated setup "the victim is humiliated on camera" is turned inside out. The bullies' own control ritual
(forced filming + supervised deletion) is what manufactures the evidence that destroys them. The Hero needs no
fighting power, no regression, no secret identity, no hacker skills. His weapon is the job they gave him and the
object they laugh at. Everything else stays genre-standard on purpose, so the twist reads clearly.

---

## 4. Series hook and serial engine

**Core engine: domino exposure (trend-brief HA Card P6).**
Each target is undone by **their own recorded words**, played **in person** to **the one audience whose opinion holds
their status** (the Lackey to his coach, the Ringleader to her parents, the protector adult to the school board).
Every clip also carries **one new piece of information** (who gave the order, who else was in the room, which adult
heard it), so each exposure opens the next target. The engine refills itself: the more he plays, the more the clips
reveal; the more they reveal, the higher the next target.

**Four gears that keep it turning past EP05:**

1. **The archive and the count.** At EP01's end the Hero states his stock: **"Forty-one clips. Five voices."** The
   number is a visible fuse. Clips get spent, never wasted; each one can only surprise once. Readers count with him.
2. **The fifth voice (mystery well, proposal for series-plotter / twist-master).** The visible bully group has four
   members. Readers will assume "five voices" means four bullies plus one. Who is the fifth? Proposed answer, paid at
   the Arc 1 / Arc 2 turn: an adult voice that is on the recordings, saying the thing that let it continue. This
   carries the series from student targets to adult complicity (D5).
3. **The hunt (counter-pressure).** Mid-season the bullies learn a copy exists and start hunting it. From then the
   Hero's tool is also his biggest risk: if the old phone is found, he loses everything. The engine switches between
   **suspense episodes** (reader knows the plan; will the bully notice?) and **surprise episodes** (prep revealed at
   the end), per D9.
4. **The cost (D6).** Some clips also hold an innocent bystander's voice or shame. Every big play forces a choice:
   whom does this hurt besides the target? At least once per arc the Hero refuses to play a clip. That refusal is what
   keeps him from becoming the bully.

**Irony that powers recurring scenes:** the school keeps putting a camera in his hands. The bullies call him
"Cameraman" as an insult; by the EP01 sting a teacher asks him to film an official event. The nickname slowly becomes
a role the adults trust, which gives him access to the rooms where faces are lost.

**Twist-type rotation (avoids one-tool fatigue by EP06+, trend-brief §6):** object -> ally -> hidden position ->
adult complicity -> the Hero's own secret. The clips stay the base; what each twist reveals changes type.

## 5. The weapon's rules (fixed; every later agent follows them)

1. **He never posts.** No Facebook, no group chat, no upload. He plays recordings **in the room**, to a specific
   audience, face to face. This is the line between justice and a viral pile-on, and it keeps the story from looking
   like a guide to filming or posting minors.
2. **He only recorded what was done to him, or in front of him.** He is a participant, not a spy. Once in Arc 1 a
   character (the good adult or the Quiet Classmate) questions whether recording was right; the story lets the
   question stand rather than citing any real law.
3. **Sound first.** The old phone lay face-down, so the archive is mostly **audio**. On the page, evidence = a voice
   coming out of a device (playback bubble with a distinct device tail) + the faces of the people hearing it. The act
   itself is never shown, filmed or replayed as an image.
4. **No hands.** He never touches anyone. Payoffs are institutional and proportional: lost position, lost trust, lost
   scholarship or exam place, a forced public apology. The Ringleader falls harder than the followers (trend-brief §7.8).
5. **Every play is planted.** No luck, no coincidence: each play has at least three earlier panels that change meaning
   once it fires (D4, HA Card P9).
6. **Short, quotable lines.** He speaks little and calmly. Count lines ("Forty-one clips." / "Day twelve.") replace
   threats.

## 6. EP01 promise (from trend-brief §4 and package A; detail belongs to episode-outliner and twist-master)

- **Cold open:** a frozen future moment: a crowd of parents and teachers listening to a voice from a speaker, the
  Ringleader's face starting to crack. "Forty days earlier." This is the Arc 1 finale, promised in the first scroll.
- **The wound:** the Hero's "job": hold the phone, film the joke, wipe it while they watch.
- **The disguised tool:** the cracked old phone (drawable signature shape: chunky, cracked corner, always face-down)
  is mocked in plain sight in B4: the main plant, shown 3 times.
- **Micro-win before the midpoint:** a small calm counter-move in front of the class (he corrects a lie with a precise
  time or a quoted line, and a witness hesitates).
- **Twist (last ~15%):** after the supervised wipe and "Nobody will believe you", alone, he presses play on the old
  phone. The Ringleader's voice comes out of a device he was never seen holding. **"Forty-one clips. Five voices."**
- **Sting:** the Ringleader's parent is invited to school for something unrelated, and a teacher asks the Hero to film
  the event. Intent resolved, outcome open (payoff-promise cliffhanger).

## 7. Season shape (proposal; arc timing is Provisional per D8, series-plotter finalizes)

**Season 1 ≈ 24 episodes, two arcs.** Clock for Arc 1: **forty days**, from EP01 to the parents' event of the cold open.

| Arc | Episodes | Target ladder | Audience feared | Arc-end turn |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arc 1 "Forty Days" | EP01-12 | Lackey exposed by EP03-04 (to his coach / team) -> a second follower flips or breaks (ally/suspect flip, EP02-06) -> Ringleader | Ringleader's parents + teachers at the parents' event (the cold open) | Ringleader's public fall at EP10-12 (never past EP15). Aftertaste: a bystander was hurt. Hook: the fifth voice is an adult. |
| Arc 2 "The Fifth Voice" | EP13-24 | The protector adult (the one who kept it quiet for the school's face or a family's money) -> the people who covered for them | School board / the wider school community | Adult complicity exposed; the one good adult acts once proof exists. Season-end twist: **the Hero's own secret**, a clip he has never played because it records his own silence when someone else was the target. |

- **Mid-season counter-twist (around EP08-10 or EP14-16):** the bullies discover a copy exists and hunt for it; the
  Hero must choose whether to use a clip that would also hurt an innocent bystander (cost beat).
- **Season 2 option (not committed):** someone else at the school has been recording too.
- **Endings rotate (D9):** win ending (a bully's face falls) / threat ending (the Hero is cornered); never two threat
  endings in a row; every cliffhanger is new information, not new pain.

## 8. Tone

- **Cold control over hot rage.** The reader feels the trap close before the bully does (dramatic irony).
- **Quiet, tense, dialogue-led.** The bullies talk a lot; the Hero talks little. Silent panels are used on purpose at
  the turn and at each play.
- **Aftertaste on every win.** A cost, a hurt witness, or a clip he chose not to play.
- **Ratio target per episode:** bullying ≤ 35-40% of panels (EP01), the rest prep, counter-moves and payoff. Suffering
  is brief; anticipation is long.
- **Not:** gleeful sadism, gore, bullying shown as cool or copyable, all-evil adults, lingering humiliation.

---

## 9. Rating rules (15+, binding for all phases)

1. **No injuries on screen.** Bullying is shown through words, faces, silence, the Hero holding a phone, and aftermath
   (a wet bag, a missing chair, a closed door). No blood, no wounds, no blows landing.
2. **No sexual content** of any kind, including as threat or joke.
3. **No self-harm or suicide**, shown, implied as an outcome, or used as a stake.
4. **The abusive act is never filmed in a panel.** Show who holds the phone and who watches; never what the lens sees.
5. **No how-to.** No step-by-step harm, no recording or hiding technique detailed enough to copy, no posting of minors.
6. **No bodily-violence payoff.** The Hero never hits back; revenge is evidence, timing and exposure.
7. **No pagodas, monks or national symbols in villain spaces; no real school, person or case.**
8. **Language:** insults are short and non-graphic; no slurs. Threats stay implied ("You know what happens.").

## 10. Target reader, comps, differentiators

**Target (Provisional, no Cambodian platform data):** Cambodian students in grades 9-12 plus readers up to ~24, on
phones, often via free Facebook-style channels, already fluent in Korean revenge drama. About 55/45 male/female:
"the weak kid outsmarts the strong" pulls male readers, "cold planned exposure" pulls female readers.

**Comps:**
- **The Glory** (drama): planned, non-violent revenge on school bullies through exposure and family face. We take its
  method and drop its long suffering (relief inside EP01).
- **Weak Hero** (webtoon): underdog intelligence against a school hierarchy. We keep the brains and remove the fists.
- **Viral Hit** (webtoon): school status through camera and video. We invert it: the camera is not the bully's stage
  but the victim's archive.

**Core differentiators:**
1. **The bully's power ritual creates the evidence** (mirror justice at its purest).
2. **Never posted, always played in person**: exposure before the people who matter, not a mob.
3. **Audio-first evidence** built for the no-text rule: voices from a device + faces.
4. **A countable weapon** (41 clips, 5 voices) that turns into a mystery (the fifth voice) and a moral cost (clips
   that hurt the innocent, and the one he will not play).

## 11. Trend justification: why this works now

The 2026 school-revenge field is led by fight-based payoffs (Viral Hit, Weak Hero, Psycho Revenge), and "weak kid gains
fighting power" and possession/regression hooks are saturated (trend-brief D1, rejected list). The year's biggest
school-justice hit drew loud "justifies violence / cathartic but empty" criticism. Meanwhile The Glory proved
mixed-gender demand for planned, non-violent exposure, but its long suffering opening was its top complaint. Play It
Back sits in the **less crowded evidence lane** (D1), delivers **mirror justice** (D2), gives **relief inside EP01**
(D3), plants every win (D4), punishes enablers while keeping one good adult (D5), and builds the aftertaste critics
asked for into the engine (D6). It also has the **lowest production risk under the no-text rule** of the three
researched packages, because its evidence is heard, not read.

## 12. Why Cambodian readers will care

- **Face is the strongest punishment.** Losing face before parents and teachers lands harder in Cambodia than any
  fight; the whole engine is built on that stage (trend-brief D2, AA watch-list #1-2).
- **Phone and video are the evidence they know.** In a reported Phnom Penh case, bullying only surfaced when a video
  reached Facebook. Readers live in a phone-video culture; here a phone finally works *for* the victim, and the Hero
  shows a more responsible path: he brings it to the room, he does not post it.
- **"Sign a letter and say sorry" is recognisable.** The failed-system beat mirrors a real kind of outcome, so the
  later "system restored" payoff (an adult finally acts) means something (AA "system restored" driver).
- **Shared school life.** Uniforms, the morning line-up, exam pressure, extra classes and group chats give "this
  happened at my school" recognition. All kept fictional; never framed as a comment on Cambodian schools in general.
- **Respect for adults.** Adult failure is specific and motivated, and one adult does the right thing; all-bad
  teachers would read as disrespectful (D5).
- **Korean revenge drama fluency.** Readers already know the genre's rhythm; one clean fresh twist is easy to grasp.
  Note: the August 2026 ministry warning about exploiting children for views is why rule §5.1 ("he never posts") and
  §9.4-9.5 are non-negotiable.

## Summary

1. Concept: "Play It Back" (Khmer proposal ចាក់ឡើងវិញ): a bullied student forced to film his tormentors' "jokes" and delete them kept every recording on a mocked old phone, and plays each bully's own words to the audience they fear.
2. 70/30: standard school-revenge codes (ringleader, followers, protective adults, exposure, mirror justice) with one flipped pillar: the victim is the camera, and the bullies' control ritual creates the evidence.
3. Engine: domino exposure (each clip exposes one target and reveals the next), a countable archive ("Forty-one clips. Five voices."), the fifth-voice mystery, the copy hunt, and a cost beat per arc; twist types rotate.
4. Rules: 15+, no injuries, sex or self-harm, the act never shown, sound-first evidence via playback bubbles; he never posts and never hits; every play is planted and every win leaves an aftertaste.
5. Season 1 ≈ 24 episodes: Arc 1 "Forty Days" (lackey -> ringleader, cold-open parents' event at EP10-12), Arc 2 "The Fifth Voice" (protector adult, adult complicity, the Hero's own unplayed clip); arc timing is provisional.
